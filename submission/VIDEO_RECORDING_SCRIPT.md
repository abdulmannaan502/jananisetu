# JananiSetu – Demo Video Narration Script and Captions

Voice: synthetic Windows TTS voice (Microsoft Zira) at rate -1. Replace with a human voiceover if the team prefers. Captions are burned into the recorded frames (bottom bar). A permanent badge reads *SYNTHETIC DATA | WHATSAPP = LOCAL SIMULATOR*.

| # | Chapter | On-screen caption | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem and introduction | JananiSetu: closed-loop maternal care continuity | Antenatal care in India still runs largely on paper cards and registers. The next visit date sits on the card, follow-up calls are made ad hoc, and missed visits are hard to see. JananiSetu is a closed-loop maternal care continuity platform. It turns existing ANC records into verified, doctor-authorised, accountable follow-up. Everything in this walkthrough uses synthetic data, and WhatsApp is a local simulator, not a live connection. |
| 2 | Problem and introduction | Assistive administration only. Doctors make every clinical decision. | Doctors stay in charge. JananiSetu never diagnoses, never scores risk and never prescribes. It helps staff keep track of what the doctor has already decided, and makes sure someone owns every follow-up that has not yet been completed. |
| 3 | Login and registration | Role-based sign-in. Each clinic sees only its own records. | Staff sign in with role-based accounts. Here, a nurse, or ANM, signs in. Each clinic can only see its own records, and what each person may do depends on their role. |
| 4 | Login and registration | Synthetic patient registration with communication consent | The nurse registers a synthetic mother. A consent choice is recorded with the registration. Without consent, no reminder is ever sent, and withdrawing consent cancels any reminders that are still pending. |
| 5 | ANC card OCR | Upload a synthetic ANC card. OCR runs locally with Tesseract. | Next, the nurse uploads an image of an ANC card. This card is synthetic. The text is read by Tesseract, an open-source OCR engine running locally, with no cloud service involved. This is the only AI component in the product. |
| 6 | ANC card OCR | Every field is shown with confidence and is accepted, corrected or rejected by a human | Every extracted field appears with its confidence and its location on the card image. Nothing is saved automatically. The nurse accepts, corrects, or rejects each field, and can mark a field illegible. Missing dates are never guessed. |
| 7 | ANC card OCR | Benchmark: 24 of 26 fields on 3 synthetic cards. Not clinical validation. | On our own benchmark, the engine matched twenty-four of twenty-six labelled fields, or ninety-two point three percent, across three synthetic cards. That is a software test on typeset text. It is not clinical validation, and real handwriting will be harder. That is why every field needs human review. |
| 8 | ANC card OCR | Confirmed record saved as a version; differences become change proposals | Once the fields are reviewed, the nurse saves the confirmed record. Any difference from the patient profile becomes a change proposal that needs approval, and it can never overwrite a doctor's plan. |
| 9 | Doctor approval and reminders | Only a doctor can authorise the next visit date | Now the doctor signs in. Only a doctor can authorise a care plan or the next visit date. Other roles receive an access error if they try. Here the doctor records the visit date for this synthetic patient. |
| 10 | Doctor approval and reminders | Simulated WhatsApp: reminders scheduled from the doctor's date, in Indian time | Reminders are scheduled only from the doctor's date, at clinic-configured offsets in Indian Standard Time, and only with consent. When we dispatch the due reminders, they go through the local simulator, not to a real phone. The banner shows that live WhatsApp credentials are not configured. |
| 11 | Doctor approval and reminders | Delivery, acknowledgement and attendance are separate states | Delivery, acknowledgement, and actually attending the clinic are tracked as separate states. A message that was delivered is not treated as a visit that happened. Failures are retried with back-off, and messages that keep failing are held for a person to review. |
| 12 | Follow-up recovery | Unanswered follow-up becomes an owned task | When a reminder is not answered, the follow-up becomes a task in the clinic work queue. A staff member is assigned, and the task stays owned and visible until it is resolved. This is the closed loop: no silent drop-off. |
| 13 | Follow-up recovery | Field visits and contact attempts. Location only with consent. | Field coordinators and nurses log visits and contact attempts. Location check-in is optional and needs explicit consent. Notes by non-clinicians cannot contain clinical observations. |
| 14 | Referral and transport | Clinician-authorised referral to a synthetic partner hospital | Referrals to partner hospitals can only be authored by clinicians. The partner facilities in this demo are synthetic records, not real agreements. |
| 15 | Referral and transport | Non-emergency transport only. Emergencies: dial 108. | Non-emergency transport is coordinated here, with verified drivers and vehicles. Emergencies are never routed through the app. Staff must call one-zero-eight by telephone, and the screen says so. |
| 16 | Budgets and accountability | Programme budget and expenses. The grant shown is a simulated demonstration. | The clinic administrator sees programme budgets and expenses in exact rupees and paise. The grant shown here is a simulated demonstration. It is not a real government sanction, and no funding has been secured. |
| 17 | Budgets and accountability | Funder report uses pseudonymous beneficiary references | Funding evidence reports use pseudonymous beneficiary references instead of names. These references are still treated as personal data, and reports are watermarked as internal drafts. |
| 18 | Budgets and accountability | Audit log: who did what, and when | The audit log records every login, upload, confirmation and action, with who did it and when, so staff ownership and decisions can be reviewed. |
| 19 | Safety and results | Phone-sized layout | The interface also adapts to a phone-sized screen, since many frontline staff work on mobile devices. |
| 20 | Safety and results | 57/57 backend tests, 7/7 browser tests. No clinical validation yet. | Freshly re-run results: fifty-seven of fifty-seven backend tests, and seven of seven browser tests, passed. The limitations are clear. All data is synthetic, WhatsApp is simulated, OCR has only been measured on three synthetic cards, there has been no clinician or patient validation, and there is no live deployment. Before any real patient is involved, JananiSetu needs a doctor workflow review, privacy and security assessment, and a small consented pilot. |
| 21 | Safety and results | JananiSetu: every doctor-authorised follow-up has an owner until it is resolved. | Existing tools reach mothers with messages, or help nurses record visits. JananiSetu connects paper records, human verification, doctor authority, reminders, and staff ownership of every unresolved follow-up in one auditable workflow. Our aim is that every follow-up a doctor has authorised is owned by someone until it is resolved. Thank you. |
